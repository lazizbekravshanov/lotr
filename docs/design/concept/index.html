The Reunited States

The real United States, redrawn as Middle-earth. A Tolkien edition of Andrej Karpathy's usaciv: the same land, the same public data and the same hex map, with new names, new art, and a stated reason for every match.

Drawn from usaciv's real grid (10,530 hexes, 25 km radius). Hover or tap anything. Gold-rimmed plates are named places; grey plates keep their real names. Diamonds are legend sites. This is a sketch of the layout, not of the final art.

The idea

usaciv draws the country like a 4X strategy map and lets every number trace back to a public source. This edition keeps all of that and adds one layer on top, the legendarium: a single data file that says what each state, city, river, wonder and fort is called in Middle-earth, and why. The engine never sees a made-up number. Switch the names to Real and you are looking at usaciv again.

  • The real numbers never change. Only names, art and stories do.
  • Lands are matched to lands, never peoples to peoples. No real community is cast as a people of the tales.
  • Places sacred to or lived in by Indigenous nations keep their own names.
  • Every match shows its basis: by place, by likeness, by name, or new-made.
  • Cities without a strong match keep their real names. The tales are told in the Common Speech.

Three ways to lay Middle-earth over America

Recommended · shown above

The applicability map

Each land goes to the realm it most resembles, by geography first and character second. The North-kingdom is the North, the South-kingdom is the South, Rohan is the grass sea, Mordor is a walled desert basin.

Every big city gets a strong identity and nobody's home is an insult. The cost: story distances bend. Gondor and Mordor are far apart.

Alternative

The mirror map

Flip Tolkien's map east to west, so the Sea is the Atlantic, and keep its order: Shire in the Midwest, Rivendell at Boulder, Moria under the Divide, the Great River is the Colorado, Minas Tirith is Phoenix, Mordor is Nevada.

The Quest traces beautifully along I-70. The cost: the East and South, where most people live, fall on Tolkien's empty lands, and Washington is no longer the White City.

Alternative

The scale overlay

Lay the true-scale outline of Middle-earth on the country and read off what falls where. It has been done as a static picture (Hobbiton lands in Kansas, Mordor on Georgia).

Honest about size, but arbitrary about meaning, and it puts Mordor on real communities. Useful only as an optional ghost outline.

Because the layout is data, changing it later costs an edit to one file, not a rebuild.

Settlements and land

  • Ten settlement styles, each in five sizes from village to great city: hobbit-holes and mills; white stone tiers; thatched halls behind a dike; havens with slender towers; lamps among great trees; gates in a cliff; a town on piles; a walled market town; a wizard's ring; a dark tower (a ruin with new farms in the Fourth Age).
  • Terrain moods on top of the real land cover: hedged patchwork, grass sea with horse herds, dark canopy, golden canopy, ash and slag, salt marsh, snowfields of the North.
  • Parchment view: the same hexes in black ink and rubric red on paper, with profile mountains and tree clumps, as a second way to look at the map.

How it is built

A fork of usaciv (MIT). It stays a static site with no framework and no build step: plain modules, Canvas 2D, and a small Python pipeline. One new builder and one new browser module carry the whole translation.

flowchart LR
  A[31 public sources] --> B[usaciv builders]
  B --> C[web/data: world, cities, states, wonders ...]
  T[pipeline/lore: hand-written tables
realms, lands, places, wonders, ages] --> L[legendarium builder + checks]
  C --> L
  L --> J[legendarium.json]
  C --> R[Renderer and panels]
  J --> M[lore.js: names, basis, culture, mood, age]
  M --> R
  R --> U[Map, City View, Council, Red Book]
  • Real identifiers (state FIPS, metro CBSA, wonder and base ids) stay the keys everywhere. Real names are never overwritten, because the pipeline's logic depends on them.
  • The builder fails the build on an unknown key, a state in two realms, or a match with no stated basis.
  • Search answers to both tongues: typing Chicago finds Annúminas.
  • All Tolkien-derived names live in that one file, so they can be swapped without touching the engine.

Build order

  1. Foundation. Import usaciv with its licence and credits, a local server, the existing tests.
  2. Legendarium. The data layer, checks, the names switch, search in both tongues. The map is already recognisably this edition after this step.
  3. Look. Fonts, palette, icons, top bar, banners by people, the Realms lens.
  4. Land. Terrain moods, mountains, rivers and coast re-drawn.
  5. Places. Settlement styles, strongholds, wonders, legend sites, folk figures.
  6. Words. The Council, City View, the Red Book and the Correspondences page.
  7. Time and roads. Ages, Great Journeys, the Parchment view.
  8. Launch. Share images, trailer with an original score, deploy to your domain.

Each step ends with a working site.
